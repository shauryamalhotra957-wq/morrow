# Morrow: 90-Day Startup Blueprint

This plan assumes the founder is a technical student who wants, within 90 days, to turn Morrow into a credible public launch, win attention from professors and recruiters, and secure the first university teaching pilot. NGO and municipal practitioners can participate as reviewers or future design partners, but the current uncalibrated release must not be sold as an operational response system. The working product has already removed most MVP engineering risk; the next job is to earn trust and prove repeated use.

## Positioning snapshot

- **Initial user:** a university disaster-management instructor or tabletop facilitator.
- **Initial buyer:** the department, continuing-education unit, simulation center, or innovation cell that funds teaching tools and facilitated exercises.
- **Category:** local-first crisis rehearsal lab with an inspectable illustrative systems model.
- **Promise:** run, challenge, compare, and export a fictional crisis exercise in one class session without an account or reliable connectivity.
- **Current boundary:** Morrow is a teaching and discussion product, not a calibrated mirror of a real jurisdiction, an operational forecast, or an emergency allocation system.
- **Differentiated combination:** offline operation, visible assumptions, seeded assumption variations, bounded portfolio comparison, and a reproducible briefing artifact in one facilitated workflow.
- **Alternatives:** dashboards monitor real conditions; spreadsheets and slide decks run exercises manually; enterprise decision platforms require integrations. Morrow should win on inspectability and session readiness, not on claims of superior forecasting.

## Phase 1 — Ideation

**Mission:** Turn a large humanitarian ambition into one sharp problem, one memorable user, and one defensible promise.

**Expected duration:** Days 1–3.

### ✅ Actions

1. Define the first user as a university disaster-management instructor running tabletop exercises—not “everyone on Earth.”
2. Write the job-to-be-done: “Help a group compare response portfolios and understand cascading consequences in one class session.”
3. Keep the category statement: **local-first crisis rehearsal lab with an inspectable systems model**.
4. Make the initial promise measurable: a facilitator can create, challenge, compare, and export a scenario in under 15 minutes.
5. Fix the flagship story: cyclone → port closure → road disruption → displacement → water/food pressure → clinic load.
6. Define the trust principles: scenarios, not predictions; assumptions visible; uncertainty shown; no autonomous decisions.
7. List the three riskiest assumptions: instructors will use it, transparent simulation is more useful than another dashboard, and an exported brief creates follow-through.

### Tools and platforms

- Morrow’s working prototype and decision brief.
- ChatGPT for interview scripts and positioning variations.
- A simple spreadsheet for assumptions, interview evidence, and decisions.
- Figma only if a facilitator workflow needs redesign; do not redraw the finished product for vanity.

### KPIs

- One primary user and one primary job-to-be-done.
- Three falsifiable assumptions ranked by risk.
- A 30-second pitch understood by 8 of 10 listeners without explanation.
- One flagship demo that runs in under three minutes.

### Founder-only insight

**Category clarity beats feature volume.** People should remember “the offline crisis rehearsal lab that makes every assumption challengeable,” not a list of dashboard capabilities.

## Phase 2 — Validation

**Mission:** Collect behavioral evidence that real facilitators will schedule, run, and repeat a Morrow session.

**Expected duration:** Days 4–14.

### ✅ Actions

1. Recruit 15 interviews without mixing the evidence: 10 instructors or tabletop facilitators for product validation, plus 3 domain reviewers and 2 student participants for safety and usability context.
2. Ask about the last exercise they ran, preparation time, weak points, decisions made, and what happened after—not whether they “like the idea.”
3. Give each participant a five-minute task without coaching: change the portfolio, explain a causal path, stress the plan, and export a brief.
4. Record task success, time, hesitation, wrong interpretations, and unprompted requests.
5. Offer a concrete pilot: one 45-minute class or tabletop exercise using a scenario tailored to their context.
6. Ask for a real commitment: a calendar slot, introduction, dataset, or written pilot acceptance.
7. Update only the problems repeated by at least three target users; log isolated requests instead of bloating the product.
8. Publish a one-page methodology summary and ask skeptical domain experts to attack it.

### Tools and platforms

- Google Forms or Tally for a short screener.
- Google Meet/Zoom plus consented notes.
- A spreadsheet with columns for pain, current workaround, task result, commitment, and quote.
- Morrow’s reproducible scenario links for identical usability tests.
- ChatGPT for interview synthesis after personally reading every note.

### KPIs

- 15 completed problem interviews.
- At least 10 users complete the core task without intervention.
- Median time to useful comparison below 10 minutes.
- Five concrete pilot commitments or calendar holds.
- At least 40% of interviewees ask to use Morrow again or share it.
- Zero critical misunderstandings that treat outputs as authoritative forecasts.

### Founder-only insight

**Compliments are not validation; inconvenience is.** A calendar booking, supplied scenario, or introduction costs the user something and is therefore stronger evidence than enthusiasm.

## Phase 3 — MVP

**Mission:** Convert the existing polished prototype into the smallest trustworthy pilot product that reliably completes the validated workflow.

**Expected duration:** Days 15–30. The technical foundation is already delivered; this phase is calibration and pilot hardening.

### ✅ Actions

1. Freeze the core loop: choose a fictional scenario → allocate budget → inspect assumptions → apply a disclosed stress case → compare objective-weighted candidates → export a brief.
2. Replace any misunderstood wording found in validation; add explanation only where users actually hesitate.
3. Have two domain experts review every coefficient label, limitation, and interpretation boundary.
4. Add one pilot-specific scenario using public, non-sensitive aggregates and mark every value observed, assumed, or modeled.
5. Run the complete automated suite, dependency audit, keyboard pass, responsive pass, offline pass, and print/PDF pass before every pilot.
6. Add anonymous, opt-in post-session feedback rather than behavioral tracking.
7. Prepare a facilitator guide, 45-minute lesson plan, answer key, and failure-recovery instructions.
8. Create a public changelog and model-version policy so results remain reproducible as assumptions improve.

### Tools and platforms

- React, TypeScript, Vite, Vitest, Testing Library, fast-check, Playwright.
- GitHub Actions for repeatable verification.
- Morrow’s evidence ledger and model card.
- Static HTTPS hosting; no backend is required for the pilot.

### KPIs

- 100% success across the critical demo journey.
- Zero high-severity accessibility or security findings.
- At least 85% domain test coverage; target already exceeded at delivery.
- Cold start below 2.5 seconds on a normal campus connection.
- Full scenario usable after connectivity is removed.
- Pilot facilitator can set up the exercise in under 10 minutes.

### Founder-only insight

**Trust debt compounds faster than technical debt in decision products.** A smaller model with visible limits will win serious users before a larger model that cannot explain itself.

## Phase 4 — Launch

**Mission:** Turn pilot evidence into a public story, repeated usage, and a clear path to paid adoption.

**Expected duration:** Days 31–55.

### ✅ Actions

1. Run three observed pilots and produce a before/after case study for each: preparation time, engagement, task completion, and facilitator outcome.
2. Capture a 90-second product film: fictional shock, cascade, intervention, disclosed stress case, candidate comparison, decision brief.
3. Publish the repository, model card, methodology, test results, and a live no-sign-up demo on the same day.
4. Launch to focused communities first: disaster-risk faculty, humanitarian-data groups, civic-tech builders, and university innovation cells.
5. Ask every pilot owner for one public quote and two warm introductions.
6. Offer a paid educational pilot: one expert-reviewed fictional scenario pack, facilitator guide, live session, private static deployment if required, and an assumptions report. Test explicit price bands with the buyer and record procurement friction. Do not sell operational outcomes from the uncalibrated model.
7. Run one live challenge where the audience chooses allocations, applies the same disclosed stress assumption, and debates the changed trade-offs.
8. Answer technical criticism publicly and update the evidence ledger; do not hide reasonable limitations.

### Tools and platforms

- GitHub, a static host, and a custom domain.
- OBS or Screen Studio for the product film.
- LinkedIn, university communities, relevant Slack/Discord groups, and direct email.
- A lightweight CRM spreadsheet until ten active opportunities exist.
- Observed-pilot scorecards and an optional post-session form. Keep the product telemetry-free; do not set funnel KPIs that the privacy model cannot measure.

### KPIs

- Three completed pilots and three usable case studies.
- At least 30 participants across observed launch sessions.
- At least 80% of observed participants complete the core rehearsal task without facilitator rescue.
- At least 40% of pilot owners schedule or request a second session within 60 days.
- 10 qualified institutional conversations.
- Three paid-pilot proposals and one signed paid pilot.
- Five organic mentions by people outside the founder’s immediate network.

### Founder-only insight

**Launches amplify evidence; they do not create it.** Lead with a measured classroom result and the assumption-challenge moment, then let the polished interface carry attention to the methodology.

## Phase 5 — Scale

**Mission:** Build a repeatable institutional product and evidence moat without turning the transparent core into a services trap.

**Expected duration:** Days 56–90.

### ✅ Actions

1. Choose one repeatable wedge from pilot data: university exercises, NGO anticipatory-action planning, or municipal resilience workshops.
2. Package three products: free public lab, paid scenario packs, and private institutional deployment with connectors and support.
3. Standardize scenario creation with a reviewed schema, provenance checklist, coefficient review, and versioned evidence bundle.
4. Create a partner program for faculty and domain experts who publish versioned, expert-reviewed scenario packs. Reserve “peer reviewed” for work that has completed a documented academic peer-review process.
5. Add team collaboration only after pilots prove a repeated need; start with signed scenario artifacts rather than accounts and permissions.
6. Establish a model review board and quarterly validity report before expanding into operational use.
7. Track cohort retention: organizations that run a second exercise within 60 days are the real product-market-fit signal.
8. Price by institutional value and support burden, not by simulated population or number of clicks.
9. Hire or recruit domain credibility before adding a large sales function.

### Tools and platforms

- Versioned public scenario registry on GitHub.
- Private static deployments or customer-controlled infrastructure.
- A lightweight CRM once the opportunity count demands it.
- Stripe or invoice billing only after the first paid pilot defines the buying process.
- Formal data-processing and security review templates for institutions.

### KPIs

- Five active institutions.
- At least 60% pilot-to-second-session retention.
- Two independently authored scenario packs.
- One repeatable sales path shorter than 45 days.
- Monthly recurring or contracted revenue sufficient to fund domain review and hosting.
- Under two founder-hours of custom work per repeated session.
- No unresolved high-severity model, security, or safety issue.

### Founder-only insight

**The moat is not the simulator UI.** It is the growing library of reviewed scenario assumption packs, provenance, outcome evidence, facilitator trust, and reproducible discussions.

# High-Impact ChatGPT Prompts

1. **Interview design**

   “Act as a skeptical disaster-management researcher. Rewrite this interview script so it tests past behavior and willingness to commit, not opinions. Flag every leading question: [paste script].”

2. **Evidence synthesis**

   “Cluster these interview notes by repeated job, pain, workaround, objection, and commitment. Preserve contradictory evidence. Do not recommend features until you identify the three riskiest assumptions: [paste anonymized notes].”

3. **Scenario review**

   “Audit this systems-pathway scenario as a modeling reviewer. For each edge, challenge direction, lag, saturation, possible confounders, and missing adverse effects. Separate published context from illustrative assumptions: [paste scenario].”

4. **Safety review**

   “Red-team this crisis decision product for over-trust, automation bias, privacy leakage, discriminatory allocation, false precision, and misuse during a real emergency. Propose product controls, not disclaimers alone: [paste workflow].”

5. **Usability diagnosis**

   “Given these task recordings and completion times, identify the smallest interface changes that remove repeated confusion. Rank by user harm and frequency; reject cosmetic ideas unsupported by evidence: [paste observations].”

6. **Pilot proposal**

   “Write a one-page university pilot proposal for Morrow. Include the learning objective, 45-minute agenda, required preparation, measurable outcomes, safety boundaries, and a direct request for a calendar slot.”

7. **Case study**

   “Turn this pilot evidence into a case study with problem, previous workflow, intervention, measured result, limitation, and participant quote. Remove hype that the evidence cannot support: [paste pilot data].”

8. **Launch story**

   “Create five launch narratives for Morrow aimed at professors, humanitarian practitioners, civic technologists, recruiters, and investors. Keep the same factual evidence but change the opening tension and call to action.”

9. **Pricing discovery**

   “Design a pricing interview for a custom scenario-pack and facilitation pilot. Test budget source, buying authority, procurement friction, alternatives, and value without asking ‘what would you pay?’”

10. **Technical review**

    “Review this pull request against Morrow’s invariants: deterministic results, bounded metrics, ordered percentiles, exact budget, validated imports, keyboard access, reduced motion, offline operation, and zero hidden model assumptions: [paste diff].”

- ✔ Launch uses pilot proof rather than unsupported hype.
- ✔ Scale follows repeat use and institutional buying evidence.
